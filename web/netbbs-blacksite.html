Blacksite is in active development. Most of the game already runs: the city hub, combat, the Lattice, factions, crews, contracts and world events are built and tested through real NetBBS doors. It is not yet a release a SysOp can install for callers. The full city, the seasons and the first release are still to come; follow along on GitHub.

The setting

Forty-one years alone, under a sky full of wreckage.

Karst is a city of four million on a limestone plateau, sealed off since the day the orbital network tore itself apart. The sky is a glittering ring of debris, and Ring-fall is loud. The city grew around Site Zero, a classified installation sunk deep into the rock, and everyone lives on top of a sealed hole whose purpose was forgotten with the people who knew it. Underneath the streets runs the Lattice, the city's surviving network, where something has been very patiently running the numbers.

You are a Wake: a freshly decanted clone with no memories, on a ninety-day lease from Sablier Biomedical, cheap labour for whichever faction gets to you first. Eight factions run the city: Halvard, Sablier, Kestrel, the Wardens, the Ninefold Choir, Red Sable, the Unmoored and the Ferrymen. The game is about what you become, and how far down you are willing to go.

Two layers, one world

Walk the streets in real time with every other caller on the node, or jack in and run the Lattice while your body waits upstairs. What you do in the network changes the street, and the reverse.

Noir, not grimdark

The city is tired and funny about it. Nobody says “hacker”; they say runner. Brand names are a language: Sablier owns your body, Kestrel owns your commute, Halvard owns the sky above the Spire.

Something below

The Blacksite has begun to exhale: warm air and old data rising up sealed shafts. Each season will open one more level, and the city's opening question will matter more.

A first session

Open your eyes slowly.

“The light's cheap and it stays on,” says Dr. Ilse Vantongeren, who runs the Wake Hall and was decanted there herself, a long time ago. You choose a template: Hardline for the front of a fight, Ghost for a rig-ready nervous system, Cantor for implants the Ninefold Choir recognises on sight, Operator for drones, trade and people. You pick a name. Your BBS handle stays visible beside it.

Vantongeren hands out the factions' recruiter cards without preference; they all resent that. Take one, or don't: it is an introduction, not a contract. Then there is a drone in the corridor that has been malfunctioning since Tuesday. It teaches the rhythm of a fight: Tab to target, F to raise your weapon, a two-second warning, F again to fire. Its shots cannot kill you. After that comes a terminal, and the Lattice.

Outside the Wake door is Clinic Row, the first contested street. Up Sablier Boulevard is Meridian Plaza, and beyond it the tram hub, the Wardens' precinct, the Terraces and Sodium Row, where Vesper brokers contracts at the Tin Halo. Trams leave on fixed 90-second boundaries. Ninety days, or grade 5 earned early through completed work, turn the lease into personhood.

Built so far

What a Wake can do in Karst

Blacksite is built in 33 steps, and each caller-facing step is tested through a real NetBBS door at 80 × 24 and a larger terminal. The first 24 are done: the foundation, the street, the Lattice, progression, and factions and conflict, plus the world events.

Share the streets

Ten connected zones across Vatside, the Core, Sodium Row and the Terraces, with scheduled trams and safe, contested and pocket zones. Walls block sight, and remembered streets stay on the map, dimmed.

Talk

Say carries 20 tiles, shout fills the zone, and whispers find anyone online. There are emotes, crew and faction chat, a sitrep of who is where, and ignore and report.

Fight, fall, decant

A new threat gets a two-second warning. A downed body can be picked up by a friend; otherwise Sablier decants you again, with a bill and a little Fade. Gear you did not secure stays in a cache for five minutes.

Feel the heat

Residents, couriers and guards go about their business. Try to attack someone in a safe zone and the Wardens warn you within two seconds, send responders and remember you for ten minutes.

Run the Lattice

Eleven district sectors, a helpless body left behind, 35 programs and 23 kinds of ICE with names like Sandman, Suture, Glasshouse and Kiln. Trace runs while you stay, black ICE reaches back up the link, and the Silt waits underneath.

Reach upstairs

A runner in a core can flip its controls: a door, a camera, a tram held at the platform. A crew at the hardware can cut a core's power instead, and the runner inside feels the floor go.

Grow

Thirty grades and twelve skill lines that rise by use. Implants with surgery risk, drugs with dependency clocks, owned drones, salvage, schematics and fabricated gear of varying quality.

Pick a side

Standing with eight factions, membership and ranks, and the Marked rule for anyone who shoots the wrong person. Crews of two to five share chat, rewards and experience. Relays are taken by holding them while a runner hacks their core.

Work the city

Contracts from Vesper and the factions, solo or with a crew, with an evidence journal for the jobs that matter. A local market and bounties. Five world events (Ring-fall, Curfew, Choir Surge, Exhale and Convoy) are forecast before they arrive.

Still to come

The rest of the city, and the descent.

Several systems above are finished and tested with clearly labelled test fixtures, because the places they belong to are not in the playable city yet: apartments in the Terraces, workshops in Old Works, salvage rings out in the Scour, and most of the sixteen relay sites. The playable city is still the ten-zone hub. The next steps are:

  • The full city. All 34 zones, including the Spire, Tramyard, the Sink, Chapel Ward, Old Works, the Undercity and four rings of the Scour outside the wall, with their vendors, faction halls, homes, relays and cores. This step is written and now in review.
  • Seasons and the Blacksite. A city-wide Depth meter that opens the first sealed level when it fills, for one resident or a crew of up to five, and a Chronicle that records what the players chose.
  • Named characters. A dialogue engine and the voices of Karst: Vantongeren, Marshal Brann, Mother Quell, Sable, the Ferry, the Dispatcher, Ninety-Nine and the rest.
  • Ready to ship. Operator tools and a SysOp guide, a load test, onboarding and in-game help, colour-blind palettes, an ANSI art pack, and a supervised NetBBS install for the first release.

The build roadmap lists every step, and the open issues show what is being worked on.
